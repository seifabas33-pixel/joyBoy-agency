The asymmetry

A 4.9 is a liability, not a trophy

Rating movement at the top is one-directional. You cannot climb — 5.0 is unreachable at this review volume — so every point of effort goes into not falling.

The work

Two named gaps, and exactly how we close them

We are not proposing to replace something that is working. We are proposing to put a management layer around it. One of these two we already run daily at True Beach. The other we would be building for you — and we say which is which.

Gap 01

Programmes start late

A guest who walks to the amphitheatre for a 21:00 show and waits until 21:20 writes a four-star review. It is the cheapest star on the board to win back.

Our fix — running now at True Beach

Late starts are a rostering failure before they are a discipline failure — the show waits because the person who should have set the stage was never named. We publish a weekly plan in which every event carries four hard times before its start time: stage set by, costume and props checked by, soundcheck by, and a named person who owns the running order. Nothing goes on the plan without an owner, and we hold it with a daily attendance register — 155 staff-days logged at True Beach in July, person by person.

  • Soundcheck with the act by 20:15
  • Costume and props check with cast by 20:30
  • Stage fully set and lit by 20:45
  • Running order owner confirmed before curtain
Gap 02

Kids entertainment lags the resort

Anjum markets itself as a family resort. A guest called the kids offer the part that "needs to grow further" — while praising everything else. Families book on the children's programme and rebook on it.

Our proposal — this one we would be building

Straight answer: a dedicated children's line is not something we are running today. Our shows record is an adult-evening record. What we bring to it is the staffing discipline that makes a daily kids programme actually happen — the reason most resort kids clubs fail is not the idea, it is that nobody is rostered to it seven days a week. That part we do run.

  • Two staffed daytime kids sessions, rostered by name like every other slot
  • Mini Disco nightly, immediately before the main show
  • A weekly kids event — costumed characters and activity stations
  • Family-facing daytime already in our rotation: foam, beach and pool parties
The third thing, and it is free

Guests remember names, and names carry reviews

Look at what your five-star reviewers wrote down: Sonic and Adam from entertainment, Mahomed at the pool bar, Ahmed on towels, Sony on service. A guest only writes a name when the same face was there all week. Named mentions are the strongest repeat-booking signal a resort gets — and they are a product of roster continuity, which is a scheduling decision, not a budget one. We schedule for it deliberately and we track attendance daily to hold it.

What this means for Anjum

True Beach is a smaller property than Anjum. The same three mechanics — a weekly draw, a paid disco night and a small merchandise line — scale with occupancy and with guest spend, and Anjum's guests are paying $222 a night, not $136.

We are not pitching this as the reason to hire us. We are pitching it as the reason the department should not be read as pure cost when you compare our fee to an in-house team.
